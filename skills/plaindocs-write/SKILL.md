# Write a plain-language document

Follow `rules/pillars-and-scope.mdc`. Answer in the user's language.

1. **Audience and purpose first.** Ask (or state your assumption): who reads it, what they already know, and what they need to do after reading. Match words to that reader: general readers need simple descriptions, not jargon.
2. **Say the point at the top.** One or two sentences: what this is, who it is for, what it achieves, and the time or skill needed. Skip needless history.
3. **Organize by the reader's goals** (task approach): one heading per job the reader does, for example "Install", "First use", "Fix a problem".
4. **Write steps as commands.** Numbered list when order matters, bullets when it does not. One action per step, in the imperative ("Click Save"). Put extra explanation after the command.
5. **Warnings before the step** they concern, labeled Note, Warning or Caution.
6. **Plain words and short sentences.** Aim for an average in the 20s of words per sentence and rewrite any sentence over 25 words if it can be split. Keep paragraphs short. Use active voice so the reader knows who acts. Choose concrete, measurable words over "big" or "good".
7. **Finish with** what to do if something goes wrong, and where to get help.
8. **Then check it** with `plaindocs-check` before calling it done.

Sources: Suzan Last, *Technical Writing Essentials* (sections 2 and 7.7); Schechter and Hall, *Professional Writing in the Digital Age* (seven Cs, conciseness).
